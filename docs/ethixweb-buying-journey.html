What the new site changesTrenchless Distribution
02

What the new site does at each step

The same seven steps, with the job the website is doing at each one.

01

Need arises

The siteLooks like the established company you are, so yours is the name he remembers when the liner runs out.

02

Search

The siteEvery product sits on its own page with its real name, so when he searches that name Google shows you and not a competitor.

03

Lands on your site

The siteSays "we supply contractors, we do not perform installations" three times before he scrolls. A pipe size picker answers "do you carry my size" in a single tap.

04

Checks the details

The siteSpec sheets, safety data and manuals attached to the product they belong to, so a crew in a crawlspace can find the cure schedule on a phone.

05

Asks the price

The siteA short request asking only name, company and phone number, with your phone number on every single screen so he can skip the form entirely.

06

Orders and collects

The siteClear pickup and freight information, and the structure to switch on online ordering later without paying to rebuild the site.

07

Comes back

The siteTraining and demo dates, named support contacts, and a question box that answers the common ones outside office hours.

The one thing to take from this

You are losing contractors at step three.

Your current site reads as though you install pipe yourselves. A contractor who believes you compete with him leaves before he ever reaches step four, and you never hear from him, so it never shows up as a lost sale. Correcting that single impression is the largest change in the new design, and it costs nothing to make.

What we need from you

Four things, and we can move the homepage from concept to build.

01

Tell us what you like, and what you do not

Blunt is genuinely more useful than polite. Nothing on the page is final.

02

Confirm your address and opening hours

We have assumed Puyallup, Washington, Monday to Friday, 7:00 to 4:30 Pacific.

03

Send your product list and spec sheets

In whatever format you already keep them. The part numbers, stock levels and prices on the page today are stand-ins.

Without written permission we will keep MaxLiner, Apex and the rest as plain text.
